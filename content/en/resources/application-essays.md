## Why essays?

Grades say what you have achieved, and essays say **who you are** and why this major. That is why most US universities ask for them, and UK universities ask for one personal statement for all your choices.

## US universities {#us}

**1. The personal essay (Common App Essay):** one essay that reaches all the universities you apply to through the Common App. You choose a question from **seven questions** for the 2026–2027 cycle, the last of which is a topic of your choice ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/essay-prompts)). The questions include: a talent or interest without which your application would be incomplete, a challenge you faced and what you learned from it, and a topic that absorbs you so much you lose track of time. Its length, at the universities that state it, is **250 to 650 words** ([example: Purdue](https://admissions.purdue.edu/apply/essay.php)).

**2. Additional essays (Supplements):** each university adds its own questions, and their number varies a lot: some universities ask for nothing, and some ask for five questions or more. The best known are “Why this university?” and “Why this major?”. The list at the bottom of the page brings together what each university asks for.

**3. The University of California (UC):** it has its own application and questions: **eight questions, of which you answer four**, and each answer is **350 words at most** ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/how-to-apply/applying-as-a-freshman/personal-insight-questions.html)). The same application is for all its campuses, such as Berkeley and UCLA.

## UK universities {#uk}

One personal statement (Personal Statement) in the UCAS application, which reaches your five choices: **three questions** about why you chose the major, how your studies have prepared you for it, and what you have done outside them, **4,000 characters** in total ([source](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)). Details are in the [guide to applying to UK universities](/resources/uk-applications#statement).

## Advice from the official bodies {#tips}

- **There is no single right answer:** the University of California says all the questions are equal, so no question has an advantage over another; what matters is that your personality, background and interests come through in your own voice ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/how-to-apply/applying-as-a-freshman/personal-insight-questions.html)).
- **Don't repeat yourself:** UCAS says the three answers are read together, so make each piece of evidence specific and linked to the major ([source](https://www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/writing-your-personal-statement/how-to-write-your-personal-statement-for-2026-entry-onwards)).
- **Write each university its own answer:** additional essays usually ask about the university itself and the major, so read the university's page before you write.
- **Start early:** early application deadlines at many universities are in October and November, as in [the application year, month by month](/grades/grade-12#year).

## Artificial intelligence and copying {#ai}

- **Common App** treats as fraud submitting a copied essay, or submitting “the substantive content or output” of an artificial intelligence platform as your own work ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/fraud-policy)).
- **UCAS:** when you apply, you confirm that your statement wasn't copied from another source, including artificial intelligence programs, and it checks for similarity between statements. Its advice: don't copy artificial intelligence text, and check any information it suggests ([source](https://www.ucas.com/undergraduate/applying-university/writing-your-personal-statement/guide-using-ai-and-chatgpt-your-personal-statement)).

Use it for brainstorming or for checking mistakes if you want, but the ideas, experiences and words must be yours.
