## What is Duolingo?

An English test from Duolingo that you take **from home on a computer, with no appointment**. It takes **about an hour**: 5 minutes for setup and identity checks, then 45 minutes for the scored test, then 10 minutes for writing and speaking samples that are sent with your result ([source](https://englishtest.duolingo.com/applicants)).

The best-known alternatives: [IELTS](/resources/ielts) and [TOEFL](/resources/toefl).

## How is it scored?

- The score is from **10 to 160**, and each skill has a score: speaking, writing, reading and listening; their average is the overall score ([source](https://englishtest.duolingo.com/test_takers/scores)).
- There are also four **Integrated subscores**, each the average of two skills: Production, Literacy, Comprehension and Conversation. Some universities set a minimum for the individual skills, not the integrated ones, so read the requirement carefully.
- The official website shows a table comparing Duolingo scores with IELTS and TOEFL ([source](https://englishtest.duolingo.com/test_takers/scores)), but the university decides the score it accepts.

## Results and validity

- Results come out within **2 days**, and you can buy a “faster results” option that reduces this to 12 hours ([source](https://testcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4481085600269-What-are-faster-results-When-will-my-results-be-available)).
- The result is valid for **2 years**, after which it can't be sent to universities ([source](https://testcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011075571-How-long-are-my-Duolingo-English-Test-results-valid)).
- You can buy **three tests in any 30 days**, and you can't start a new test before the previous result arrives ([source](https://testcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011075451-How-often-can-I-take-the-Duolingo-English-Test)).

## Duolingo or IELTS? {#which}

It depends on the university, not the test. Many US universities accept Duolingo, but many universities in the UK and Australia don't mention it among their tests. The list at the bottom of the page brings together what each university's page says.

- **Waaid programs:** some of its programs accept it. For example: the bachelor's in mineral science engineering with Ma'aden asked for “a Duolingo test with an overall score of 105, with no skill below 100”, or IELTS or TOEFL ([source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/paths/path-waaid/), our translation). Each program has its own requirements.
- **The university itself:** if you don't find your university in the list, search for “English language requirements” on its website.

## How to start?

1. Create an account on the [official website](https://englishtest.duolingo.com/applicants) and try the **free practice test**: it takes about 45 minutes, gives you an approximate range for your score, and you can retake it as often as you like ([source](https://testcenter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011076591-The-Practice-Test-Prepare-to-take-the-Duolingo-English-Test-and-estimate-your-score)).
2. Make sure your universities accept it, and with what overall score and skill scores.
3. Take the test well before the application deadlines, so you have time to retake it.
