## What is IELTS?

An English test asked for by most universities that teach in English (the common alternative: [TOEFL](/resources/toefl)). For university study choose **IELTS Academic**, which is meant for bachelor's and graduate study according to the [official website](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test).

It takes 2 hours and 45 minutes, at a test centre or online, and measures four skills:

- **Listening**: about 30 minutes.
- **Reading**: 60 minutes.
- **Writing**: 60 minutes.
- **Speaking**: 11 to 14 minutes.

## How is it scored?

Each skill gets a score from 0 to 9, and the overall score is **the average of the four skills, rounded to the nearest half band**. An example from the official website: 6.5, 6.5, 5.0 and 7.0 average 6.25, so the overall score becomes 6.5.

- **When do results come out?** 1 to 5 days for the computer test, and 6 to 8 days for the online test.
- **How long does it stay valid?** The test's organisers advise treating it as valid for **2 years**, and some institutions accept a longer period. Check with the place you will apply to.

## What score do you need?

There is no single score for everyone. These numbers are from their official sources:

- **Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques Scholarship Program, foundation year (bachelor's):** at least 5.5, with a high school average of 90% or more. [Source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/conditions/info-4/)
- **Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques Scholarship Program, studying in China (bachelor's):** at least 4.5. [Source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/conditions/info-4/)
- **Waaid track:** each program has its own score. The programs announced in 2026 asked for 5.5 to 7 (or TOEFL 100). [Source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/paths/path-waaid/)
- **KAUST (KGSP), last announced cohort:** 7.0 in IELTS Academic (or 90 in TOEFL iBT) guarantees you an interview, and those who don't reach it may still have their application considered. [Source](https://kgsp.kaust.edu.sa/prospective-students)
- **The university itself:** each university sets its own score, and it is the one that decides your admission. Look on the International admissions page of the university's website.

**Important for Arruaad and Emdad:** the scholarship **doesn't cover an English-language year on its own** in countries that teach in English, such as the US and the UK ([source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/conditions/)). A **foundation year** for the bachelor's, however, is accepted on condition of IELTS 5.5 or more and an average of 90% or more ([source](https://sites.moe.gov.sa/scholarship-program/conditions/info-4/)). And an admission conditional on an English test isn't mentioned in the conditions. So make sure you reach the university's score **before** you need your admission to be final. Details are on the [program page](/programs/custodian-scholarship).

## How to start?

1. Try a **free official practice test** to know your level now: [official preparation resources](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources).
2. Find the score you need from the universities and the program you are aiming for.
3. Focus your practice on your weakest section.
4. Book your test well before the application deadlines, so you have time to retake it if you need to. Booking is on the [official booking page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/booking-your-test).
