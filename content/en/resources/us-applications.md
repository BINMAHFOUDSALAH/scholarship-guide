## Which system do you use? {#which}

The university decides how you apply to it, and you will find it on each university's page on Wadih under “Application platform”. Most cases are one of three:

- **Common App:** one application you send to many universities. You fill in the shared information once, then what each university asks for.
- **The University of California application (UC):** a separate system for the University of California campuses (Berkeley, UCLA, San Diego and others). One application for all campuses, but **the fee is per campus** ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/apply-now.html)).
- **The university's own portal:** such as MIT. The instructions are on the university's website.

## What does the application contain? {#contents}

**On the Common App** ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/first-year-students/)):

- Sections you fill in: Profile, Family, Education (your grades and courses), Testing, Activities and Writing.
- **A personal essay:** you choose a topic from **7 topics** for 2026–2027 ([the topics](https://www.commonapp.org/apply/essay-prompts)).
- **Recommendations:** the school counsellor sends the school report and the transcript, and each university decides the number and type of teacher letters. Ask for the letter at least three weeks before the deadline ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/static/088c92645a5827f61016fd81ac42b3f4/Resource_FY_RecProcess_ENG_2025.06.25_1.pdf)).
- **University questions:** some universities add their own essays or short questions. What each university asks for, and how to write: [admission essays](/resources/application-essays).

**On the University of California application (UC):**

- **Personal questions:** you answer **4 of 8** questions, with 350 words per answer at most ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/how-to-apply/applying-as-a-first-year/personal-insight-questions.html)).
- **You don't send recommendation letters** with the application, and a campus may ask for them later ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/apply-now.html)).
- **The application period for fall 2027:** from 1 October to 30 November 2026 ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/how-to-apply/applying-as-a-first-year/dates-and-deadlines.html)).

The English test, the SAT and each university's dates are on its page on Wadih.

## Application fees and waivers {#fee-waiver}

Answer these questions in order. **A waiver is never guaranteed**, and answer its questions with your family's real financial circumstances.

1. **Which system do you use?** The Common App, UC or the university's portal (the first section).
2. **You are an international applicant.** A Saudi student studying in Saudi Arabia applies as an international student, and some universities treat US and international students differently for fees and waivers.
3. **Does the waiver program cover you?**
    - **Common App:** an international student may be eligible. Most of its conditions are US programs (such as reduced-price school meals and the Pell grant), and they also include **a letter supporting your request from a school official, counsellor, financial aid officer or community leader**. You request it from: My Common Application > Profile > Common App fee waiver, then read the conditions and sign ([source](https://appsupport.commonapp.org/applicantsupport/s/article/What-do-I-need-to-know-about-the-Common-App-fee-waiver)). Your school counsellor is asked to confirm your eligibility ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/static/f9d54dd41c76d04ef3e91779d03f7b93/Resource_FY_AppFees_ENG_2025.06.25_0.pdf)).
    - **University of California (UC):** the fee is **95 dollars per campus** for international applicants, and the waiver program is **only for US citizens, permanent residents and those covered by AB540 benefits** ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/apply-now.html)). The 80-dollar fee is **a reduced fee, not a waiver**, and applies to international students enrolled at a US educational institution ([source](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/admission-requirements/international-applicants/applying-for-admission/)).
4. **Does the university accept this waiver?** Each university has its own policy: some don't accept the Common App waiver, some accept it for US students only, and some have their own waiver ([source](https://appsupport.commonapp.org/applicantsupport/s/article/What-do-I-need-to-know-about-the-Common-App-fee-waiver)). Look at “Application fee” on the university's page on Wadih, or the table below, then check on the university's website.
5. **If it isn't accepted, what's the alternative?** Some universities have their own waiver that an international student can request, and more than 500 universities on the Common App don't charge international students an application fee, according to the Common App ([source](https://www.commonapp.org/static/f9d54dd41c76d04ef3e91779d03f7b93/Resource_FY_AppFees_ENG_2025.06.25_0.pdf)). Each university's fee appears on the Common App under My Colleges.
